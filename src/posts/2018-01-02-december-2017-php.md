The pages of the calendar has turned, and our dates now start with 2018, at least for those of us following ISO-8601. Still in this new year, I'm planning to keep doing my monthly lists of interesting tech-related articles to read.

Starting from today I'm starting a new job as a backend developer at the Danish food waste startup <a href="http://toogoodtogo.co.uk/">Too Good To Go</a>, so the content of these summaries might change a bit since reading will likely follow the technologies I use in my daily life, but the main focus point will still be PHP.

<h2>Symfony 4 released</h2>
Just over a month ago the next major Symfony version, <a href="https://symfony.com/blog/hello-symfony-4">Symfony 4, was released</a>. Jani Tarvainen listed some of the new major changes in his article <a href="https://www.symfony.fi/entry/four-things-i-like-about-symfony-4">4 things I like about Symfony 4</a>. On the Symfony blog they also posted a <a href="https://symfony.com/blog/symfony-2017-year-in-review">year in review</a>.

Besides a new major version, Symfony also announced their new <a href="https://symfony.com/blog/the-diversity-initiative">Diversity Initiative</a>. I'm looking forward to see what this will entail, and how it will work out.

<h2>Secure coding practices</h2>
The beginning of a new year is often a time of reflection and making changes. Over at the Paragon IE they released a long-form <a href="https://paragonie.com/blog/2017/12/2018-guide-building-secure-php-software">Guide to building secure PHP software</a> that can hopefully inspire some people to pick the habit of focusing more on security when writing software.
